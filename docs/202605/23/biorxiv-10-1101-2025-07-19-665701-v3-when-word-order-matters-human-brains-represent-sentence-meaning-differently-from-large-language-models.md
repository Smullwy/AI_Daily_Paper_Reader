## 摘要
基于Transformer架构的大型语言模型如今已能产生类似人类的语言。但它们对人类语言意义的编码与处理方式，是否与人类相同？本文通过分析30名参与者在阅读108个句子时的7T功能磁共振成像（fMRI）数据来探讨这一问题。这些句子经过精心设计，以分离句子结构与词汇意义，从而检验Transformer对句子意义的表征是否与大脑形成的表征相似。我们发现，虽然Transformer模型比完全忽略词序的模型更匹配大脑表征，但所有Transformer模型的表现整体上都较差。此外，Transformer模型显著逊色于那些明确编码词间结构关系的模型。我们的研究结果揭示了大脑中句子表征的本质，突显了句子结构的关键作用。同时也对Transformer表征句子意义的方式与人脑相似这一观点提出了质疑。

## Abstract
Large language models based on the transformer architecture are now capable of producing human-like language. But do they encode and process linguistic meaning in a human-like way? Here, we address this question by analysing 7T fMRI data from 30 participants reading 108 sentences each. These sentences are carefully designed to disentangle sentence structure from word meaning, thereby testing whether transformer representations of sentence meaning resemble those formed by the brain. We found that while transformer models match brain representations better than models that completely ignore word order, all transformer models performed poorly overall. Further, transformers were significantly inferior to models explicitly designed to encode the structural relations between words. Our results provide insight into the nature of sentence representation in the brain, highlighting the critical role of sentence structure. They also cast doubt on the claim that transformers represent sentence meaning similarly to the human brain.
