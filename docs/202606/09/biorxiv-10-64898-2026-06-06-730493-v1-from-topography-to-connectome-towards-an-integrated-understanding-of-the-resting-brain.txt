Abstract 22 

As the field expands from early research into the human connectome, there has been a fast 23 

expansion in the number of analytical approaches to study resting state functional MRI (rsfMRI) 24 

data. With increasing focus on individual differences, topographical brain maps of spatial 25 

organization have emerged in addition to traditional functional connectomes. Here, we developed 26 

a deep -learning model to embed maps of network topography and faithfully translate to 27 

individualized connectomes. Results confirmed the validity of the surface vision transformer based 28 

on reconstruction accuracy (0.73±0.09) and accurate topography -to -connectome translation 29 

(0.43±0.08). Importantly, translated connectomes retained identifiability and brain -cognition 30 

associations. These findings establish a direct mapping from spatial topography to connectomes 31 

that can be used to integrate scientific insights across rsfMRI sub -fields. This is an important step 32 

towards broadening our conceptualization of the connectome and supporting a broader integration 33 

of findings to inform a complete understanding of the human connectome. 34

Introduction 40 

The human connectome was initially proposed as a way to illustrate how functional brain states 41 

emerge from their structural substrates (1). Specifically, the goal of the human connectome was to 42 

provide a robust structure -function mapping of the human brain by combining information about 43 

white matter structural connectivity (SC) from diffusion weighted imaging with functional 44

20 years, the human connectome has helped characterize various properties of SC such as network 46

structure -function mapping revealed large overlaps of pathways with SC across the brain (8–10 ).48 

However, not all FC connections are bound by known cortico -cortical pathways, largely due to FC 49 

representing statistical co -activation patterns across the cortex (9, 11 ). Consequently, the 50 

connectome has offered a flexible definition of FC, contributing to its rising popularity in brain -51 

wide association studies, and resulting in a rapid expansion of approaches to study FC. 52 

Generally, FC is studied using group -derived parcellations to extract time series and 53 

calculate region -by -region temporal correlations (12 ). Beyond these functional connectomes, 54 

studies have expanded the quantification of rsfMRI to derive individualized spatial topography 55 

maps (13 ). This expansion of possible brain representation (i.e., low -dimensional summary 56 

features) derived from rsfMRI scans has provided novel insights into individual differences in 57 

functional organization (14 ). However, this analytical variability also catalyzed divergent 58 

methodological silos, limiting replicability and clinical applications (15 ). Direct comparisons of 59 

results have proven helpful to map out the effects of analytical variability in related domains 60 

including fMRI preprocessing and relevance to behavior (14 , 16 ). However, leveraging direct 61 

comparisons to integrate FC findings is challenging due to fundamental mismatches in the type of 62 

brain representations (temporal connectomes vs topography maps), as well as their dimensionality 63 

and spatial boundaries. Despite these fundamental mismatches that obscure straightforward 64 

comparison, both brain representations are derived from the same rsfMRI scans and may therefore 65 

capture overlapping information. 66 

There is an important need to integrate findings regarding functional organization across 67 

different rsfMRI brain representations, such as region -to -region temporal connectomes and 68 

topography maps. Such integration of rsfMRI findings is necessary to guide correct mechanistic 69 

interpretations, gain a comprehensive understanding of the connectome, and inform best practices 70 

for analytical approaches in clinical trials. Recently, a toolbox was developed to assess spatial 71 

correspondence between group -level parcellations used to define temporal connectomes and group -72 

level topography maps (17 ). These results are useful for comparing group -level spatial parcellation 73 

definitions, potentially in combination with standardized nomenclature (18 ). However, this spatial 74 

correspondence toolbox was not designed to integrate findings regarding individual differences. 75 

Other studies have anchored comparisons of individual differences across rsfMRI brain 76 

representations based on their associations with behavior (19 –21 ). These studies showed 77 

remarkable consistency in the behaviorally -relevant information captured regardless of the 78 

parcellation and indicated 60% shared variance between temporal connectomes and topography 79 

maps (21 ). Although these results are valuable to map behaviorally relevant shared variance 80 

between brain representations, brain wide associations have been shown to capture a relatively 81 

minor portion of individual differences, such that the use of behavior to anchor comparisons has its 82 

own limitations (22 ).83 

In our prior work, we leveraged topological data analysis to identify how brain 84 

representations compare in preserving inter -subject variability features on a shared manifold (23 ),85 

revealing overlap between topography and amplitudes but dissimilarity between topography and 86 

partial FC connectomes. Others have opted to facilitate comparisons across brain representations 87 

by developing transforms. For example, leveraging the optimal transport problem, parcellated time 88

encoder framework when embedding cortical surface maps, allowing us to achieve topography -to -104 

connectome translation accuracy on par with prior connectome -to -connectome translations while 105 

preserving downstream brain wide association results. These findings therefore establish an 106 

efficient direct mapping from spatial topography to connectomes of rsfMRI, providing a path to 107 

integrate insights and results across the two. 108

Results 110 

We trained and tested our rsfMRI topography -to -connectome translation framework (Fig. 1) using 111 

data from the Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) cohort with a site -based split of 112 

training (N train = 7104), validation (N val = 920), and test (N test = 624). Model performance was 113 

primarily assessed using the within -subject correlation between measured and translated outcomes 114 

after subtracting the training group mean. 115 

The result section consists of four main parts. In the first part, we validated the SiT encoder 116 

architecture by assessing reconstruction accuracy in a topography -to -topography framework for 117 

individual -specific topography maps. In the second part, we applied the validated encoder into a 118 

topography -to -connectome framework to translate from a 15 -dimensional set of ICA topography 119 

maps to a 100 -dimensional connectome. Our goal was to translate these individual -specific 120 

topography maps into corresponding individualized connectomes. In the third part, we therefore 121 

explicitly tested the preservation of individual differences by assessing to what degree the translated 122 

connectomes retained subject identifiability and brain -cognition associations. A translation model 123 

is only practically useful if it translates well to different settings. Therefore, in the fourth part, we 124 

determined the generalizability of the topography -to -connectome model to different connectome 125 

dimensionalities (100 vs 300), and parcellations (Schaefer vs Glasser). Full details on the 126 

architecture, hyperparameters, optimization, and assessments are available in the Materials and 127 

Methods. 128

Translating from spatial topography to connectomes 152 

We combined the SiT encoder with a linear decoder to translate from topography network maps 153 

(all 15 ICA maps) to a connectome defined as the full pairwise bivariate correlations using the 100 -154 

dimensional Schaefer atlas (31 ) (Materials and Methods). Similar to the reconstruction application, 155 

we trained the model to translate demeaned connectomes to avoid regression to the group mean. 156 

Translation accuracy was assessed with the demeaned versions of both the measured upper triangle 157 

of the connectome and its translated counterpart. 158 

Translation accuracy in the held -out test dataset was on average r = 0.43±0.08. Connectome 159 

translations for representative subjects in the top 10%, median, and bottom 10% are shown in Fig. 160 

3. Other metrics of model performance are shown in supplementary Table 1, and model 161 

performance as a function of training mini -batches in training and validation data is shown in 162 

supplementary Fig. S2. Taken together, these results confirm the ability of the SiT encoder -decoder 163 

framework to translate from spatial FC topography to individualized connectomes. In the next 164 

section, we explicitly evaluated to what degree individual differences were retained after 165 

translation. 166

Preserving connectome individual differences 168

connectomes. As such, we tested the preservation of individual differences across translated 170 

connectomes. Specifically, we performed two different tests of subject identifiability and two 171 

separate brain -cognition association analyses. Firstly, we assessed the average rank percentile, 172 

which compares how a measured connectome and its corresponding translation compare against all 173 

other translated connectomes. To successfully preserve connectome individual differences, any 174 

given measured connectome should be more correlated with its translated counterpart than with as 175 

many other translations as possible. Average rank percentile measures this fraction, where a ratio 176

Discussion 232 

In this work, we introduced a novel topography -to -connectome translator model that can map from 233 

individualized spatial maps to analogous connectomes, while preserving individual differences in 234 

finger printing and downstream brain -cognition analyses. We first validated the proposed spatial 235 

topography encoder, showing high and consistent reconstruction accuracy across brain maps, 236 

including both primary and higher association networks. When testing the full topography -to -237 

connectome translator, translated connectomes demonstrated consistent and robust preservation of 238 

measured connectomes including preserved subject identification and brain -cognition associations. 239 

Overall, this paper contributes to the connectomics field by integrating individual difference 240 

findings from spatial topography analyses of rsfMRI data with findings from FC connectomics. 241 

Our topography -to -connectome translator achieved a performance of r = 0.43 (demeaned 242 

correlation between measured and translated connectomes), well within the range of previous 243 

connectome -based translation efforts. For example, Krakencoder was optimized to map from one 244 

connectome type to another, using a many -to -many approach by embedding various kinds of 245 

connectome “flavors” and assessing reconstruction and translation accuracy among them (25 ). In 246 

their study, the accuracy for reconstructions and translations ranged r = 0.30 -0.70, except for partial 247 

FC connectomes (typically r = 0.07 -0.30). Others have attempted to translate across SC and FC by 248 

using a more complex generative model. For example, authors in (26 ) trained an adversarial 249 

generative model to translate from structural and functional connectomes, with performance 250 

accuracies typically ranging from r = 0.34 -0.43. Using our SiT encoder -decoder, translation 251 

accuracy somewhat diminished when translating to higher resolution connectomes but was stable 252 

between different atlas types (Fig. 5). The small decrease in translation accuracy for the Schaefer -253 

300 and Glasser -360 parcellations may result from having the linear decoder infer more elements 254 

for higher parcellation sizes. Additionally, translations to higher dimensional connectomes may be 255 

impacted by residual between -subject misalignment. Future work may wish to explore how 256 

improved alignment using multimodal surface matching (32 , 33 ) prior to the calculation of 257 

topography maps may impact translation accuracy. 258 

Our topography -to -connectome direction presents a different task from the connectome -to -259 

connectome translations discussed above because the source is a non -euclidean object being 260 

mapped onto a Positive Semi -Definite connectome form. In fact, our previous work suggested that 261 

spatial maps and connectomes have predominantly diverging inter -subject variability features, 262 

making them difficult to compare ( 23 ). Despite this diverging variability, our translator converged 263 

to a non -linear mapping that directly translated from post -preprocessed dual regressed ICA brain 264

brain representations of rsfMRI data that could be leveraged to enrich and integrate our 269

vertices within a parcel are averaged to define the time -series for that parcel. The spatial definitions 271 

of parcels are often defined from a group -average atlas, whereas topography maps like ICA, define 272 

varying functional boundaries across the cortex and across subjects. Therefore, for any given group -273 

averaged parcel, differing functional boundaries across subjects systematically impact the parcel 274 

time series and thus establish an association between parcel timeseries and functional boundaries 275 

in ICA that can be exploited by our topography -to -connectome translators ( 35 ). 276 

Importantly, the translated connectomes retained individual difference information. 277 

Identifiability was strong, suggesting the usability of translated connectomes for finger printing 278 

(36 ). In fact, both univariate and multivariate brain -cognition associations preserved enough 279 

information regarding individual differences, to the extent that both measured and translated 280 

connectomes were strongly correlated in their univariate associations and achieved similar 281 

multivariate brain -cognition associations. These results suggest that connectome -wide individual 282 

differences were retained despite a slight drop in edge -wise individual differences. This advantage 283 

of multivariate over univariate performance may be driven by relatively higher levels of noise at 284 

the edge level and/or shared variance (e.g., redundancy of information) across the full multivariate 285 

connectome ( 37 ). 286 

Notably, translating to partial connectomes had the lowest performance of all connectome 287 

translations. This result is somewhat surprising given that partial connectomes have been shown to 288 

benefit from noise reduction ( 38 ). However, as mentioned previously, similar drops in performance 289 

were observed in the Krakencoder ( 25 ), and partial connectomes have been shown to have lower 290 

test -retest reliability ( 39 ), potentially due to instability in regularization ( 40 ). Furthermore, ICA 291 

derived topography maps represent whole brain connectivity for any given network by grouping 292 

similar vertices, which more closely resemble full correlation connectomes. Partial correlation FC 293 

connectomes, instead, regress out all other time series before calculating a pairwise edge, resulting 294 

in a sparser FC matrix. This difference may explain why translation accuracy was higher for full 295 

correlation than partial correlation connectomes. Further work is needed to integrate partial 296 

connectome results with ICA and full correlation FC connectomics. 297 

Integrating results across rsfMRI studies is crucial to inform translational research of brain 298 

diseases. To date, practical applications of rsfMRI in clinical settings are relatively limited. This 299 

dissonance between rsfMRI analyses and clinical work arises from limitations in brain wide 300 

association ( 22 ), non -standardized nomenclature ( 18 ), disagreement across analyses pipelines ( 16 ), 301 

and flexibility in choice of brain representation ( 15 ). The topography -to -connectome translator 302 

model addresses the latter issue by assuming brain representations share underlying latent features 303 

that can be used to tether them. Recently, this perspective has been expanded on to enable a 304 

“multiverse approach” without the high computational burden ( 34 ), by embedding various kinds of 305 

analysis pipelines into a lower dimensional latent space and using a deep learning approach to 306 

sample and learn across this subspace. Learning inter -relationships between pipelines from this 307 

low -dimensional space allowed authors to approximate a wide spectrum of rsfMRI analytical 308

Although our results revealed the ability to translate from topography and connectomes 312 

while retaining individual differences, there are several limitations to discuss. Firstly, deep learning 313 

models require optimization of a large number of parameters, which requires a big training dataset. 314 

Although we achieved reasonable performance using ABCD data, it is possible that we have not 315 

yet hit the ceiling of translation accuracy. In future work, we plan to expand to the UK Biobank 316 

cohort (which now includes N=100,000, enabling a more than 10 -fold increase in our current 317 

training sample) to test this hypothesis. Secondly, there are many other types of topography brain 318 

maps (additional dimensions and methods), many other connectomes, and additional sources of 319 

analytical variability (e.g., variation resulting from data acquisition and preprocessing pipelines). 320 

Although we tested the ability to translate to different connections, future work will be needed to 321 

expand the translations to a broader set of topography and connectome brain representations. 322 

Specifically, in future work, we plan to expand into a comprehensive set of ‘ NeuroTranslators’ that 323 

separately learn mappings from one topography based representation to some connectome profile, 324 

such that each separate model can be fused to reach an abstracted unifying latent space capturing 325 

underlying statistical properties of rsfMRI data that facilitate direct translation and comparison. 326 

In summary, we demonstrated the ability to faithfully translate individual FC connectomes 327 

from rsfMRI topography maps. These results broaden our conceptualization of the connectome and 328 

emphasize the relevance of rsfMRI brain representations that are not traditionally considered as 329 

‘connectomes’ to the field of connectomics. As such, we believe a broader integration of findings 330 

will be valuable to inform a complete understanding of the human connectome, not only within the 331 

context of other types of connectomes, but beyond that and onto other domains of analyses. With 332 

this in mind, our topography -to -connectome mapping seeks to resolve a goal identified in the 333 

original proposal of the human connectome, namely the creation of a publicly accessible 334 

standardized data format. In the presence of expanding analytical approaches to study rsfMRI, this 335 

standardized data format goal can be reenvisioned through comparison and integration facilitated 336 

by translation. 337

Dataset 340 

The Adolescent Brain Cognitive Development (ABCD) cohort is a publicly available dataset with 341 

a large sample size of typically developing children (N=8,648) from ages 9 - 11 recruited across 21 342 

different sites in the United States (41 –43 ). Data were acquired across three types of 3T scanners 343 

(Siemens Prisma, General Electric 750, and Philips) running standard echo -planar imaging 344 

sequence protocol (TR=800ms, TE=30ms, flip angle=52°, multiband factor=6, 2.4mm isotropic 345 

voxel resolution). ABCD data were preprocessed using the ABCD -BIDS pipelines (42 ), including 346 

standard frame -to -frame head motion correction, nonlinear alignment to standard space, Freesurfer 347 

extraction of the cortical ribbon, and formatting to grayordinate format. Data cleanup for rsfMRI 348 

furthermore followed the ‘DCAN -BOLD Preproc’ module, including regression of white matter, 349 

CSF, mean CIFTI timeseries, the six motion parameters and their volterra expansion, bandpass 350 

filtering (0.008 -0.09 Hz), and motion censoring. Each subject had four 5 -minute resting state scans, 351 

which when concatenated resulted in a total of 20 minutes of resting state data (41 ). To account for 352

inverse on the full correlation matrix, which is analogous to using an L2 -regularized inverse. 366

Embedding and Translating Spatial Topography Maps 368 

Both reconstruction and translation used the same SiT -based encoder paired with a linear decoder. 369 

We did not use a convolutional encoder because these have been shown to perform less well for 370 

cortical surface data ( 30 , 45 ). The reason that transformers outperform convolutional neuronal 371 

networks (CNNs) can be traced to their powerful self -attention learning mechanism ( 29 , 46 ) that 372 

encodes understanding of long -range context via modelling the co -variance of different features 373 

across the entire surface. This is necessary for modelling cognition since these processes recruit 374 

distributed networks spread over the entire cortical surface. CNNs cannot achieve this because they 375 

purposely learn highly localized filters. More recently, designs of generalizable 'foundation 376 

models' (47 , 48 ) evidences that - with enough data - transformers are now broadly seen as the 377 

architecture of choice for complex image understanding tasks. 378

Edge -wise associations between cognition and connectomes 461 

For all connectomes, univariate brain -cognition associations were computed as follows. Across 462 

subjects, each edge in the upper triangle of measured and translated connectomes were correlated 463 

with cognitive measures, separately. Measured univariate associations and translated univariate 464

Fig. 1. Schematic for ICA embeddings into latent space and linear decoders. (A) Topography -694 

to -topography models embedded the vertex space of each subject's normalized topography map 695 

(separate models for each of 15 ICA networks) into a lower dimensional triangular tessellation that 696 

formed the input to the SiT. Output of the SiT was mapped into a latent vector from which a linear 697 

transformation reconstructs triangular patches that were compared to the measured normalized 698 

topography input patches. ( B) The translation models were trained using the normalized measured 699 

topography maps of all 15 ICA networks as input. Each topography map was first resampled into 700 

the same non -overlapping triangular tessellation patches used in (A), which were combined across 701 

all 15 spatial brain maps. The models then projected these patches into a latent space and used a 702 

final linear transform to decode the upper triangle of the demeaned FC connectome, which was 703 

compared to the measured demeaned FC connectome. Importantly, the group mean was removed 704 

from the inputs to the models and the targets for reconstruction or translation to avoid regression to 705 

the mean and encourage sensitivity to individual differences. Abbreviations: Reshape (RS), Surface 706 

Vision Transformer (SiT), Linear Layer (LN). 707
