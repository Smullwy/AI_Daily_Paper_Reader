# Abstract 1Macaque brain MRI is central to translational and comparative neuroscience, yet multi-site, longitudinal, and 2cross-species analyses are hindered by a lack of unified, automated structural processing tools. Existing 3pipelines, mostly adapted from human neuroimaging or restricted to fragmented steps, fail to provide robust 4surface-volume representations across heterogeneous acquisitions and developmental stages. Here we 5introduce MacaSurfer, a fully automated, containerized framework for unified surface-volume mapping of the 6macaque brain across the lifespan. MacaSurfer features components tailored for macaque anatomy: a tissue 7segmentation model, a tissue-guided bias-field correction method optimizing structural mapping from T1-8weighted images alone, topology-aware surface reconstruction, and surface-aware volumetric registration. 9Validated on 1,346 imaging sessions from 965 macaques across 39 international sites (spanning 2 weeks to 23 10 years of age), MacaSurfer demonstrated exceptional anatomical consistency, test-retest precision, and 11 robustness against image degradation. Leveraging MacaSurfer-derived morphometry, we established 12 normative trajectories from 835 macaques, providing a standardized reference for downstream individualized 13 deviation analysis. MacaSurfer is openly available with source code, containers, and pretrained models, 14 offering a reproducible ecosystem to accelerate developmental, translational, and comparative neuroimaging. 15

# Introduction 1Macaques play an important role in translational and comparative neuroscience. Their phylogenetic proximity 2to humans, together with the feasibility of experimental manipulation, makes them particularly valuable for 3studying cortical organization, brain development, disease mechanisms and cross-species homology 1–3 . Within 4this framework, structural MRI provides an essential in vivo tool for comparing individuals, tracking structural 5change over time and relating macaque brain organization to human neuroimaging phenotypes 4 . Therefore, 6macaque structural MRI is not only a tool for describing anatomy, but also a framework for organizing 7downstream analyses across subjects, studies and species. 8Despite this importance, macaque MRI datasets are typically acquired in relatively small cohorts and 9under site-specific experimental conditions 5 . Substantial variation in scanner hardware, acquisition protocols, 10 spatial resolution, image quality and coil-related intensity inhomogeneity further complicates data processing 11 and limits direct comparability across studies 6 . The scientific value of existing datasets, and their utility for 12 future work, therefore depends not only on data acquisition itself, but also on whether diverse datasets can be 13 processed with a unified and reliable workflow that generates anatomically consistent representations across 14 studies 7 . Such a workflow is essential for enabling direct comparison, reproducibility and the cumulative reuse 15 of macaque neuroimaging data. 16 However, this level of integration remains lacking in existing approaches, which are largely adapted 17 from human MRI analysis or focused on isolated processing steps. While tools such as CIVET-Macaque 8 and 18 PREEMACS 9 have advanced automated cortical surface extraction, and recent deep learning methods have 19 improved brain extraction and tissue segmentation 10,11 , these developments do not yet provide a unified 20 preprocessing workflow that can robustly handle cortical surface representations with volumetric anatomy 21 across heterogeneous acquisitions, image qualities and developmental stages. 22 We therefore developed MacaSurfer, an end-to-end macaque-specific framework that integrates 23 preprocessing, cortical and subcortical segmentation, topology-aware surface reconstruction, multi-atlas 24 parcellation, subject-to-template mapping, morphometric and volumetric quantification, visual quality control 25 and normative reference analysis within a single reproducible workflow. # MacaSurfer establishes a unified framework for macaque surface–volume 2

# mapping 3We first sought to establish MacaSurfer as a unified framework for structural MRI analysis of the macaque 4brain, rather than as a tool for an isolated preprocessing step. To resolve the systemic methodological 5fragmentation in non-human primate brain imaging research, we built MacaSurfer as a standardized, fully 6automated and high-throughput computational workflow that spans preprocessing, cortical and subcortical 7segmentation, surface reconstruction, parcellation, subject-to-template mapping, morphometric quantification, 8visual quality control and downstream normative analysis within a single reproducible pipeline. By organizing 9cortical surface geometry and volumetric anatomy within a common representation, the framework supports 10 anatomically consistent downstream analysis across subjects, studies and imaging conditions. Built upon a 11 containerized Nextflow orchestration engine 12 , MacaSurfer achieves end-to-end automation from raw 12 structural inputs to standardized morphometric outputs, supports process-level caching and parallel execution 13 across local workstations and high-performance clusters, and is BIDS-compliant 13 — automatically 14 configuring its workflow based on available inputs while retaining the flexibility for targeted customization in 15 atypical or challenging cases. 16 To maximize empirical robustness across heterogeneous datasets and complex edge cases, MacaSurfer 17 integrates an automated quality control system coupled with specialized remediation protocols. The workflow 18 records all processing parameters and generates comprehensive visual reports at every critical stage (brain 19 masking, tissue segmentation and surface fitting), enabling rapid and transparent cohort assessment. When 20 confronting common failure modes (e.g., skull-stripping errors, white-matter defects) or severe structural 21 anomalies (e.g., ventricular enlargement, extreme artifacts), MacaSurfer's modular architecture provides 22 targeted remediation procedures; these individual modules can be iteratively refined and re-executed without 23 recomputing the entire pipeline, drastically reducing the cost of manual intervention. # MacaSurfer generalizes across sites, ages and challenging anatomical 2

# conditions 3To evaluate the generalizability and robustness of MacaSurfer, we applied it to a large multi-center collection 4comprising 1,346 imaging sessions from 965 rhesus macaques acquired at 39 international research sites. This 5cohort represents a wide spectrum of acquisition heterogeneities, demonstrating the workflow's immunity to 6variations in scanner hardware, coil configuration and imaging protocol (Fig. 2a). 7As illustrated by the representative reconstructions from 24 sites, MacaSurfer produced anatomically 8consistent cortical surfaces despite substantial heterogeneity in image contrast and resolution. The workflow 9further exhibited remarkable stability across the macaque lifespan: high-fidelity reconstructions were 10 successfully obtained from 2-week-old infants — whose brain images typically feature low tissue contrast due 11 to incomplete myelination — through to aged adults (23 years; Fig. 2b). The framework's ability to maintain 12 reconstruction fidelity in early postnatal brains is particularly notable, as infant macaque MRI presents unique 13 challenges (reduced gray–white matter contrast, higher water content and smaller brain volume) that frequently 14 lead to under-segmentation or topological defects in conventional pipelines. 15 We then asked whether MacaSurfer could handle challenging "edge cases" in which reliable cortical 16 surface reconstruction may be intrinsically difficult or inappropriate, because distorted anatomy or degraded 17 image quality can compromise tissue boundaries and surface topology. We therefore evaluated whether the 18 framework could still provide anatomically meaningful structural outputs in scans exhibiting atypical 19 ventricular enlargement, scan-related anatomical deformation or severe motion artifacts. The macaque-specific 20 tissue segmentation module produced plausible cortical, subcortical and ventricular labels in these challenging 21 scans, enabling reliable identification of major anatomical compartments even when downstream surface 22 reconstruction was limited or required cautious interpretation (Fig. 2c). In subjects with enlarged ventricles, the 23 segmentation preserved the separation between ventricular space, periventricular tissue and cortical gray 24 matter; in scans affected by deformation or motion, the model still recovered the main tissue compartments 25 despite local signal degradation. These results indicate that MacaSurfer provides robust structural segmentation 26

# repeated scans 6To quantify robustness and reproducibility, we evaluated MacaSurfer under three complementary conditions: 7controlled image degradation, repeated acquisitions from the same individuals, and a T1w-only versus 8combined T1w/T2w comparison. 9

Robustness under controlled image degradation. To quantify the stability of MacaSurfer under 10 suboptimal imaging conditions, we performed a stress test using 12 repeated scans of a single subject from the 11 Monkey Kingdom dataset. We generated degraded T1-weighted variants via controlled perturbations — bias-12 field distortion, Gaussian and Rician noise, and spatial downsampling — and compared MacaSurfer's 13 performance with CIVET-Macaque 8 without prior denoising, to ensure a stringent evaluation. Applicability to T1w-only acquisitions . We further asked whether MacaSurfer could maintain high-1fidelity structural mapping from T1-weighted images alone. We compared surface reconstructions derived 2from T1w-only inputs against those obtained from combined T1w/T2w inputs in 80 subjects across 12 3independent imaging sites. Vertex-wise paired t-tests (FDR-corrected, p < 0.01) revealed no significant 4differences in mean cortical thickness across 97.12% and 97.79% of left and right hemisphere cortical vertices, 5respectively, and complementary Kolmogorov–Smirnov tests showed that cortical thickness distributions were 6statistically indistinguishable across more than 99.9% of the cortex in both hemispheres. Other metrics (e.g., 7sulcal depth) exhibited slightly higher rates of significant difference (~11–13%), but cortical curvature and 8thickness remained remarkably consistent across modalities (Supplementary Fig. 11; Supplementary Result 3). 9This parity supports the use of MacaSurfer in datasets where multimodal structural acquisitions are unavailable 10 or inconsistent, including historical archives and simplified acquisition protocols. 11

# Discussion 1This study establishes MacaSurfer as a comprehensive computational platform that addresses the long-standing 2methodological fragmentation in macaque neuroimaging 5,15 by unifying surface and volume processing within 3a single, bidirectional information flow. Unlike traditional pipelines adapted from human-centric architectures 416 that treat volumetric segmentation and surface reconstruction as independent, feedforward stages, 5MacaSurfer implements an integrated framework where volumetric deep-learning priors stabilize surface 6initialization, while cortical geometric constraints concurrently refine volumetric registration. Built upon a 7containerized Nextflow orchestration engine 12 , the framework achieves end-to-end automation from raw 8structural inputs to standardized morphometric outputs. # Framework architecture and design 5MacaSurfer is a modular, configuration-driven pipeline for macaque cortical reconstruction that supports both 6multimodal (T1w + T2w) and unimodal (T1w-only) inputs (Fig. 5b). The workflow is organized around four 7integrated stages (Fig. 1a). (1) Data Preparation: an automated orientation correction module resolves 8coordinate header errors prevalent in non-supine macaque acquisitions by registration-based alignment to the 9MEBRAINS template, followed by within-subject rigid alignment across repeated scans. (2) Quality 10 

Enhancement: a 3D deep-learning engine jointly segments 18 tissue classes; these semantic priors drive a 11 Tissue-Guided Bias Field Correction algorithm that decouples coil-induced intensity inhomogeneities from 12 biological signals, recovering white-matter contrast in low-signal temporal and occipital regions. (3) Surface 13 

Reconstruction: signed distance functions (SDFs) and template-derived priors constrain a topologically 14 correct white-matter skeleton, recovering thin white-matter strands frequently lost to partial volume effects in 15 voxel-wise segmentation. Following white-surface reconstruction, volume-to-surface mapping combined with 16 vertex connectivity is used to extract middle-wall voxels, after which the pial surface is reconstructed—refined 17 on T2w data when available. (4) Spatial Transformation: Surface-Aware Volumetric Registration constrains 18 3D deformation fields with explicit cortical geometric correspondences, improving cross-subject alignment in 19 complex sulcal regions compared to conventional intensity-based registration. Surface alignment is achieved 20 via MSM spherical registration to the template surface. The detailed workflow is summarized in Fig.1a, and 21 algorithmic components are described in subsequent sections. 22 A central design principle of MacaSurfer is the establishment of a unified anatomical reference that 23 links template-space atlas definitions, subject-level label generation, and population-level normative modeling 24 within a single consistent framework. # MacaSurfer Technical Details 14 Although MacaSurfer underwent three rounds of iterative development, its overall architecture and processing 15 logic remained largely unchanged. This was because, from the first version, the pipeline was designed around 16 the most common and challenging problems in macaque structural MRI processing. Subsequent iterations 17 therefore focused on refining targeted solutions to part of problems, rather than restructuring the entire 18 workflow. 19 

Preprocessing and Volumetric Analysis 20 Built on the unified framework architecture, we first perform standardized preprocessing and volumetric 21 analysis to ensure reliable input for subsequent surface reconstruction. This section describes the key steps that 22 correct spatial inconsistencies, extract brain tissues, and refine volumetric signals. 23 

Automated Orientation Correction. To address the high frequency of coordinate header errors in 24 macaque datasets 38 , each skull-stripped brain was rigidly registered to the MEBRAINS template 28 using 25 FLIRT 39–41 to infer the native image orientation, which was then written to the input volume. This procedure 26 was validated across all PRIME-DE acquisition sites 5 by manual inspection. The detailed algorithmic 27 workflow and registration settings are provided in Supplementary Note 1. 28 

Volumetric Registration. Volumetric registration in MacaSurfer comprises three steps: (1) intra-29

Unified Tissue Segmentation and Brain Masking. Accurate isolation and classification of brain 8tissues are critical for cortical surface reconstruction. MacaSurfer employs a unified multi-class tissue 9segmentation strategy in which brain extraction is implicitly defined by the union of predicted anatomical 10 labels. The default model jointly segments cortical gray matter, white matter, cerebrospinal fluid, cerebellum, 11 brainstem, and multiple subcortical structures, generating both a high-fidelity brain mask and anatomically 12 informative labels for downstream analyses. 13 To address the scarcity of annotated macaque MRI data, tissue segmentation was developed using an 14 iterative, pipeline-preserving bootstrapping strategy. The downstream reconstruction workflow (bias-field 15 correction, volumetric registration, surface initialization, and surface refinement) was kept fixed while only the 16 segmentation component was iteratively improved. Initial labels were obtained by combining deep learning– 17 based cortical tissue segmentation 10 with atlas-informed subcortical labeling after volumetric registration. 18 Cases with suboptimal masks or tissue labels were manually corrected and reprocessed, yielding a curated set 19 of anatomically consistent, surface-informed labels for model training. 20 Using this strategy, we assembled a training set of 2,157 macaque MRI scans from 39 acquisition 21 centers. The final segmentation model is based on a SwinUNETR-B architecture 18,19 initialized with self-22 supervised pretraining and fine-tuned for 18-class tissue segmentation. Complete details of the training dataset, 23 model architecture, augmentation strategies, and performance metrics are provided in Supplementary Note 3. 24

Surface Reconstruction and Refinement 5Based on the high-quality volumetric outputs from preprocessing, we proceed to reconstruct and refine the 6cortical surfaces with strict topological constraints. This section presents the core algorithms that generate 7accurate white and pial surfaces while addressing macaque-specific anatomical challenges. 8

White Surface Initialization and Topology Correction. To generate the initial white surface, we 9construct a volumetric mask that provides a closed and topologically valid boundary for surface initialization. 10 This mask is derived from the aseg volume produced by the hybrid tissue segmentation strategy, after all 11 images have been conformed to the MEBRAINS template space 28 . By explicitly incorporating subcortical 12 structures into the initialization mask, the resulting white surface maintains a globally closed topology suitable 13 for subsequent deformation and refinement. 14 This volumetric representation ensures anatomically consistent boundaries at the cortex–subcortex 15 interface and prevents topological defects that can arise when white matter is considered in isolation. Thin and 16 elongated white matter structures—particularly in the occipital lobe and anterior frontal lobe—may be 17 underrepresented in voxel-wise segmentation due to partial volume effects 46 . To recover these regions, we 18 integrate template-derived white matter priors via a Signed Distance Function (SDF) 47 computed from the 19 template white matter surface projected into subject space. This skeleton acts as a hard topological constraint, 20 enabling recovery of white matter strands missed by voxel-wise segmentation. 21 For hemisphere separation, we replaced the conventional sagittal cutting-plane strategy with a subject-specific 22 hemispheric mask generated using FreeSurfer’s make_hemi_mask, which estimates a symmetric half-way 23 space via inverse-consistent registration 48 to avoid inaccuracies from planar midsagittal approximations 49,50 .24

Topological Correction and Medial Wall. Inaccuracies in volume-based atlas-to-subject nonlinear 6registration can cause the initial cortical surface mask (i.e., lh.cortex.label) to erroneously extend into the 7medial wall. Such misclassification between cortex and medial wall regions can adversely affect subsequent 8surface-to-surface registration, ultimately compromising the accuracy of mapping cortical parcellations onto 9individual subject surfaces 54 . To ensure an accurate and robust medial wall definition, we generate a precise 10 midline mask by projecting the nonlinear hemisphere separation boundary (from volume space) onto the white 11 surface. We then treat the surface as a graph and extract the largest connected component to define the valid 12 cortical mantle, followed by a hole-filling step that removes spurious cortical fragments in the medial region 13 and generates a topologically closed cortex mask. Visual inspection of the PRIME-DE dataset 5 confirmed that 14 this algorithm produces anatomically correct cortical masks with no obvious failures. 15 

Multi-contrast Pial Surface Deformation. The pial surface is generated by iteratively expanding the 16 white matter surface toward the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) boundary. Accurate placement is particularly 17 challenging in deep sulcal regions where gray–CSF contrast in T1w images is often weak, and in high-field 18 acquisitions where vascular structures can confound cortical boundaries. 19 To improve robustness, we adopt an iterative, multi-contrast pial surface reconstruction strategy inspired by the 20 Human Connectome Project pipelines 16 , in which different contrast cues are progressively integrated to guide 21 surface placement. 22 In the initial iteration, CSF information derived from the aseg volume is used as a soft constraint to 23 modulate local T1w intensity profiles, encouraging the evolving pial surface to expand sufficiently into sulcal 24 depths. Subsequent refinement iterations incorporate T2w contrast (when available) or a synthetic T2w-like 25 contrast generated from T1w via intensity inversion to fine-tune pial surface placement. 26 Through this multi-stage refinement process, multiple candidate pial surfaces are generated, reflecting different 27

Surface-aware Volumetric Registration.
