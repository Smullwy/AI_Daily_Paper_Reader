## 摘要
言语产生需要喉上发音器官（如唇、舌和软腭）与喉部发声控制的协调配合。已知皮层感觉运动区编码语音特征，但小脑如何表征言语以及小脑表征如何整合到皮层-小脑环路中仍知之甚少。通过使用7T功能性磁共振成像和表征相似性分析，我们研究了在分别改变发音部位和嗓音起始时间的显性音节产生过程中言语产生的神经表征。我们在皮层和小脑言语区域都发现了可靠、音节特异的活动模式。腹侧初级感觉运动皮层通过发音部位区分音节，而背侧感觉运动皮层对嗓音起始时间敏感，这与发音和发声控制之间的功能分离一致。我们还发现了编码语音特征的次级言语区。具体而言，顶叶岛盖对发音部位和嗓音起始时间均表现出敏感性，使其成为言语运动网络中的一个整合节点。在小脑中，我们发现了上、下两个言语区，它们的表征几何结构没有差异。令人惊讶的是，小脑的音节表征与顶叶岛盖中的表征最为相似，而不是初级运动皮层，这两个脑区都编码两种语音特征。这些发现表明，小脑言语表征并非仅由单个初级感觉运动区决定，而是与顶叶岛盖中的表征更为一致，提示小脑言语区有助于发声和发音的协调。

## Abstract
Speech production requires the coordinated control of the supralaryngeal articulators (e.g., the lips, tongue, and soft palate) together with laryngeal control of phonation. While cortical sensorimotor regions are known to encode phonetic features, how the cerebellum represents speech and how cerebellar representations integrate within cortico-cerebellar circuits remain poorly understood. Using 7T functional MRI and representational similarity analysis, we examined neural representations of speech production during overt syllable production varying in place of articulation and voice onset time. We found reliable, syllable-specific activity patterns across both cortical and cerebellar speech regions. Ventral primary sensorimotor cortex distinguished syllables by place of articulation, whereas dorsal sensorimotor cortex showed sensitivity to voice onset time, consistent with a functional dissociation between articulatory and phonatory control. We also found secondary speech areas that encoded phonetic features. Specifically, the parietal operculum showed sensitivity to both place of articulation and voice onset time, positioning it as an integrative node within the speech motor network. In the cerebellum we found superior and inferior speech areas, which did not differ in their representational geometry. Surprisingly, the cerebellar syllable representations were most similar to those found in the parietal operculum rather than in primary motor cortex, with both regions encoding both phonetic features. These findings indicate that cerebellar speech representations are not solely determined by a single primary sensorimotor area but instead align more closely with the representations in the parietal operculum, suggesting that cerebellar speech areas contribute to the coordination of phonation and articulation.
