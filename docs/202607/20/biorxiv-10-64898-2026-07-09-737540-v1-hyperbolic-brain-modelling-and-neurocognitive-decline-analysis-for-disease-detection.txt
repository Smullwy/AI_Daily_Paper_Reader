## Abstract

distortion, undermining neuroimaging diagnostic 3

frameworks. While hyperbolic models like the 4

Poincar´ e ball preserve these nested topologies, they 5

three neurocognitive disorders. 21 

## 1 Introduction 22 

Modern neuroimaging diagnostic frameworks formulate 23 

complex structural and functional networks of the brain in 24 

graphical forms, but embedding these highly hierarchical ar-25 

chitectures into standard Euclidean space introduces severe 26 

structural distortions. Recent frameworks have been using 27 

hyperbolic geometry models like the Poincar´ e ball [Yang et 28 

al. , 2026], to preserve these nested topologies with minimal 29 

distortion. However, these baseline models rely on curved 30 

geodesics and complex M¨ obius operations, leading to signif-31 

icant computational overhead. In this work, we propose a 32 

non-Euclidean approach based on the Beltrami-Klein (Klein) 33 

ball model for a robust neurocognitive decline analysis. By 34 

mapping hyperbolic geodesics directly to Euclidean straight 35 

lines, the Klein model simplifies distance computation to dot 36 

products, substantially improving computational efficiency. 37 

We evaluate our framework against existing Poincar´ e and 38 

Lorentz baselines across three major neurocognitive disorders 39

## 2 Background 44 

Neurocognitive decline diseases, particularly, Alzheimer’s 45 

disease and related dementias; pose a serious global public 46 

health challenge, affecting an estimated 57 million people 47 

around the world and approximately 9.8 million new cases 48 

emerging annually [World Health Organization, 2025; GBD 49 

2021 Dementia Collaborators, 2025]. This epidemiologi-50 

cal crisis disproportionately affects low- and middle-income 51 

countries (LMICs); which accounts for over 60% of the 52 

global burden, resulting in substantial socioeconomic disrup-53 

tion, diminished patient autonomy and emotional caregiver 54 

burden [World Health Organization, 2025]. While conven-55 

tional clinical assessments (e.g., MMSE, MoCA) face subjec-56 

tive variability and pronounced misdiagnosis rates in primary 57 

healthcare settings, modern diagnostic frameworks increas-58 

ingly use advanced neuroimaging modalities; including struc-59 

tural MRI for automated cortical atrophy quantification and 60 

Positron Emission Tomography (PET) for tracking metabolic 61 

decline and toxic protein accumulations [Livingston and oth-62 

ers, 2024]. Although integrating these high-resolution imag-63 

ing techniques yields an objective diagnostic accuracy of 64 

80–90% under expert supervision, their widespread deploy-65 

ment remains severely bottlenecked by extreme infrastruc-66 

tural costs, geographic disparities, and complexities in clin-67 

ical interpretation introduced by overlapping pathologies in 68 

the aging brain [World Health Organization, 2025]. 69

Klein model applies projective non-Euclidean geometry to 71

a nested, modular architecture with exponential path expan-74 

sion; Euclidean space distorts these graph properties. 75 

By projecting these structures onto the Klein disk (or n-76 

dimensional unit ball), researchers can preserve hierarchical 77 

topologies while benefiting from straight-line geodesics that 78 

radically simplify neural simulations and network calcula-79

## 3 Methodology 89 

In this work, we propose a Klein-based approach for the anal-90 

ysis of neurocognitive decline in the early detection of neuro-91 

logical disorders. We have evaluated our framework against 92 

Poincare- and Lorentz-based approaches on Schizophrenia, 93 

Parkinson’s disease and Alzheimer’s disease; using datasets 94 

obtained from publicly available sources, including ADNI. 95 

We cite [Yang et al. , 2026] as our baseline paper for our pro-96 

posed work. 97 

3.1 Preliminaries and Problem Formulation 98 

Let GF = (V, AF , XF ) and GS = (V, AS , XS ) denote 99 

the functional connectivity (FC) and structural connectivity 100 

(SC) brain graphs for a given subject, respectively. Here, 101 

V denotes the set of N brain regions-of-interest (ROIs), 102 

AF , AS ∈ RN ×N are the corresponding adjacency matrices, 103 

and XF ∈ RN ×N , XS ∈ RN ×3N are node feature matrices 104 

encoding FC and SC characteristics, respectively. 105 

Given a collection of subjects with corresponding brain 106 

graphs and diagnostic labels y ∈ { 0, 1} (or {0, 1, 2} for three-107 

class tasks), the objective is to learn a mapping that accurately 108 

predicts the disease status of each subject. 109 

Our framework builds upon the Hyperbolic Kernel Graph 110 

Fusion (HKGF) model proposed by Yang et al. [Yang et al. ,111 

2026], which originally operates in the Poincar´ e ball model 112 

of hyperbolic space. Rather than modifying the HKGF archi-113 

tecture, we have investigated three distinct hyperbolic geom-114 

etry models — Poincar´ e ball, Lorentz hyperboloid, and Klein 115 

(Beltrami-Klein) ball — as the underlying geometric space. 116 

Our results show that the Klein model consistently outper-117 

forms the original Poincar´ e geometry while achieving higher 118 

computational efficiency. 119

3.8 Training and Implementation Details 246 

All models use the Adam optimiser with cosine annealing 247 

and linear warm-up (warmup epochs = 10 , T0 = 25 ). The 248 

curvature parameter is fixed at c = 0.001 following the 249 

sensitivity analysis in [Yang et al. , 2026]. Other hyperpa-250 

rameters are selected via 5-fold cross-validation grid search: 251 

learning rate ∈ { 10 −3, 5 × 10 −4, 3 × 10 −4}, weight decay 252 

∈ { 5 × 10 −4, 10 −3}, dropout ∈ { 0.3, 0.4, 0.5}, batch size 253 

∈ { 8, 16 }. Hidden dimension M = 64 , HNN hidden dimen-254 

sion = 32 , λ = 0 .01 . All experiments are run for 200 epochs 255 

with early stopping (patience = 40 ) based on validation AUC. 256 

Performance is reported as mean ± standard deviation across 257 

5-fold cross-validation repeated 5 times with different ran-258 

dom seeds, using seven metrics: AUC, ACC, F1, BAC, SEN, 259 

SPE, and PRE. 260

## 5 Discussion 263 

The above results highlight that the proposed Klein-based 264 

approach surpasses all the existing baselines and provides a 265 

more effective representation for neurocognitive disease anal-266 

ysis. 267 

1. Klein HKGAT achieves the best overall performance 268 

across all three diseases. It achieves the highest or joint-269 

highest AUC on all three datasets — SCHZ (83.92), PPMI 270 

(97.33), ADNI (92.14), making it the most consistent and 271 

powerful geometry-backbone combination evaluated. 272 

2. The Klein geometry is the only model that scales to 273 

all dataset sizes without memory failure. While Lorentz 274 

and Poincar´ e HKGAT both crashed on ADNI (170 sub-275 

jects) due to RAM limitations, Klein HKGAT completed 276 

successfully. This is directly attributable to Klein’s ef-277 

ficient tanh/atanh operations vs cosh/sinh/acosh chains in 278 

Lorentz/Poincar´ e which generate larger intermediate tensors. 279 

This scalability is particularly advantageous for large clinical 280 

datasets. 281 

3. Hyperbolic models consistently outperform Eu-282 

clidean baselines on all datasets. On SCHZ, Klein HKGAT 283 

improves the best Euclidean baseline from 79.68 to 83.92 284 

AUC (+4.24). On ADNI, it improves AUC from 87.51 to 285 

92.14 (+4.63 AUC). These results support the hypothesis that 286 

brain connectivity graphs have an inherent hyperbolic struc-287 

ture. 288 

4. GAT dominates GCN across all geometries and 289 

datasets. On every geometry where both variants were evalu-290 

ated, HKGAT surpasses HKGCN — indicating that attention-291 

based neighbourhood aggregation captures the heterogeneous 292 

brain region connections more effectively, than uniform con-293 

volution. 294 

5. Difficulty of classification varies across diseases. 295 

PPMI (Parkinson’s disease) is the easiest classification task 296 

with all models achieving AUC above 96.9 — suggesting that 297

## 6 Conclusion 318 

In this work, we presented a systematic investigation of three 319 

hyperbolic geometry models — Poincar´ e ball, Lorentz hyper-320 

boloid, and Klein (Beltrami-Klein) ball — as the underlying 321 

geometric space within the Hyperbolic Kernel Graph Fusion 322 

(HKGF) framework [Yang et al. , 2026] for automated neu-323 

rocognitive decline detection from multimodal brain imaging. 324 

Experiments were conducted across three clinically distinct 325 

neurological diseases — schizophrenia (SCHZ), Parkinson’s 326 

disease (PPMI), and Alzheimer’s disease (ADNI) — span-327 

ning 54 to 170 subjects, making this the first multi-disease, 328 

multi-geometry comparative study of hyperbolic brain graph 329 

neural networks. 330 

Our key findings are as follows. First, the Klein HK-331 

GAT model consistently achieves the highest AUC across 332 

all three diseases (SCHZ: 83.92%, PPMI: 97.33%, ADNI: 333 

92.14%), establishing it as the most accurate and generalis-334 

able geometry-backbone combination tested. Second, Klein 335 

is the only geometry where both HKGCN and HKGAT vari-336 

ants successfully complete on all dataset sizes, while Lorentz 337 

and Poincar´ e HKGAT both encounter out-of-memory fail-338 

ures on the larger ADNI dataset ( N = 170 ). This scal-339 

ability advantage stems directly from Klein’s use of tanh 340 

and tanh −1 operations, which avoid the intermediate ten-341 

sor expansions introduced by cosh , sinh , and arccosh chains 342 

in competing geometries. Third, all hyperbolic models out-343 

perform their Euclidean counterparts (SVM, RF) across all 344 

datasets, validating the hypothesis that brain connectivity 345 

graphs possess intrinsic hierarchical structure that is better 346 

captured in negatively curved space. Fourth, the attention 347 

mechanism (HKGAT) consistently outperforms graph con-348 

volution (HKGCN) across all geometries and datasets, con-349 

firming the importance of adaptive neighbourhood aggrega-350 

tion for modelling heterogeneous brain region interactions. 351 

Fifth, a sensitivity-specificity tradeoff is observed across ge-352 

ometries: Klein HKGAT prioritises high specificity (SPE 353 

90.65% on ADNI, 93.36% on PPMI), making it well-suited 354 

for population-level screening, while Lorentz HKGCN offers 355 

a more balanced SEN/SPE profile suited to clinical diagnosis 356 

where minimising false negatives is critical. 357 

Taken together, these findings establish the Klein model as 358 

the recommended hyperbolic geometry for brain graph neu-359 

ral networks, particularly in resource-constrained and large-360 

dataset clinical settings. Future work will explore learnable 361 

curvature schedules robust to small sample sizes, integration 362 

with standard neuroanatomical atlases (e.g., AAL), and ex-363 

tension to longitudinal multimodal datasets to track disease 364 

progression over time. 365
