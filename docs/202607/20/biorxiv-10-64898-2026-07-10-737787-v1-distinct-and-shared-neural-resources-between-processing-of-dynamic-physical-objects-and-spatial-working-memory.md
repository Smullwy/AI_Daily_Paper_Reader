## 摘要
在人类大脑中，当人们对物理世界进行推理（例如追踪物体、做出预测）以及当人们执行各种高要求任务（例如工作记忆、运动抑制）时，额顶叶皮层的一些部分会做出反应。在这里，我们使用功能神经成像来解决一个悬而未决的问题，即相同的神经资源是否支持这两种心理功能。对 28 名健康成年人（平均年龄 = 26.5 岁；17 名女性；26 名右利手）进行功能性磁共振成像（fMRI）扫描，同时让他们（i）追踪物理对象（相对于社会代理）的动态，以及（ii）执行困难（相对于简单）的空间工作记忆任务。对于每个参与者，我们识别了在每个任务中最大程度参与的功能感兴趣区（fROI）（物理 > 社会；困难 > 简单），并研究了它们在保留任务中的参与情况。我们报告了三项证据，表明物理 > 社会 fROI 在空间工作记忆任务中被募集。首先，在物理（相对于社会）加工过程中最大程度参与的功能感兴趣区，在困难（相对于简单）空间工作记忆期间反应同样强烈。其次，在一组区域中具有强困难 > 简单反应的人，在另一组区域中也往往具有强物理 > 社会反应。第三，在物理 > 社会 fROI 中表现出强困难 > 简单反应的人，但在困难 > 简单 fROI 中没有，在扫描仪中的空间工作记忆表现更强。这些发现表明，优先对物理（相对于社会）刺激做出反应的额顶叶皮层部分参与了物理推理之外的功能：要么是专门的空间加工，要么是一般需要注意力的任务。

## Abstract
In human brains, portions of the frontoparietal cortex respond when people reason about the physical world (e.g. track objects, make predictions), and when people engage in a variety of demanding tasks (e.g. working memory, motor inhibition). Here, we use functional neuroimaging to address an open question about whether the same neural resources support both mental functions. Twenty-eight human adults (Mage = 26.5y; 17 female; 26 right handed) were scanned using functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) while they (i) tracked the dynamics of physical objects (vs social agents), and (ii) performed a hard (vs easy) spatial working memory task. For each participant, we identified functional regions of interest (fROIs) that were maximally engaged by each task (physical > social; hard > easy), and studied their engagement in the held out task. We report three pieces of evidence that physical > social fROIs are recruited during the spatial working memory task. First, fROIs maximally engaged during physical (vs social) processing responded just as strongly during hard (vs easy) spatial working memory. Second, people with strong hard > easy responses in one set of regions also tended to have strong physical > social responses in the other set. Third, people with strong hard > easy responses in physical > social fROIs, but not in hard > easy fROIs, performed more strongly at spatial working memory in the scanner. These findings suggest that portions of the frontoparietal cortex that preferentially respond to physical (vs social) stimuli are involved in functions beyond physical reasoning: either spatial processing specifically, or attentionally demanding tasks in general.
