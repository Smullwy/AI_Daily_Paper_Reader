## 摘要
学习阅读第二语言需要大脑将一种新的书写系统整合到已建立的母语阅读网络中，然而这一过程如何重塑视觉词形区(VWFA)仍知之甚少。本研究利用功能性磁共振成像(fMRI)和被动观看范式，系统考察了以汉语为母语的儿童在第二语言(英语)读写能力获得的三个不同阶段：未开始二语阅读的儿童、二语阅读初学者和二语阅读高级者中，VWFA对汉语(L1)和英语(L2)词汇的反应如何演变。我们结合了单变量激活分析、表征相似性分析和监督机器学习分类，以检验关于VWFA重组的两种理论模型：叠加模型(Overlay Model)，预测在L2反应出现时L1反应保持稳定；以及竞争模型(Competing Model)，预测神经资源从L1到L2的竞争性重新分配。我们发现，尽管VWFA已经对L1词汇表现出稳健的选择性反应，但在未开始二语阅读的儿童中缺乏对L2词汇的选择性，而这种选择性在初学者中稳健出现，同时L1词汇的选择性始终稳定。表征相似性分析进一步揭示，VWFA内稳健的L2词汇表征仅在儿童开始学习阅读二语后才出现，而L1词汇表征在所有三组中保持稳定。最后，监督机器学习分析成功地基于L2而非L1的激活模式区分了三组二语读写能力水平，表明仅VWFA对L2的反应就足以捕捉儿童二语读写能力获得的阶段，而对L1的反应则对二语学习经验不敏感，从而不支持竞争模型关于神经资源从L1竞争性转移的预测。总之，这些发现支持叠加模型，表明VWFA在其现有的皮层资源中整合新的书写系统，而不会损害L1的文字加工。

## Abstract
Learning to read a second language requires the brain to incorporate a new writing system into an already established native-language reading network, yet how this process reshapes the Visual Word Form Area (VWFA) remains poorly understood. Using fMRI with a passive viewing paradigm, we systematically investigated how VWFA responses to Chinese (L1) and English (L2) words evolved across three groups of native Mandarin-speaking children at distinct stages of L2 literacy acquisition: L2 pre-readers, L2 beginning readers, and L2 advanced readers. We combined univariate activation analyses, representational similarity analysis, and supervised machine learning classification to investigate two theoretical accounts of VWFA reorganization: the Overlay Model, which predicts stable L1 responses as L2 responses emerge, and the Competing Model, which predicts competitive reallocation of neural resources from L1 to L2. We found that although the VWFA already exhibited robust selective responses to L1 words, L2-word selectivity was absent in L2 pre-readers but emerged robustly in beginning readers, with L1-word selectivity remaining stable throughout. Representational similarity analysis further revealed that robust L2 word representations within the VWFA emerged only after children began learning to read L2, while L1 word representations remained stable across all three groups. Finally, supervised machine learning analyses successfully discriminated among the three L2 literacy groups on the basis of L2 but not L1 activation patterns, indicating that VWFA responses to L2 alone were sufficient to capture children's stages of L2 literacy acquisition, whereas responses to L1 were insensitive to L2 learning experience, providing no support for the Competing Model's prediction of competitive neural reallocation away from L1. Together, these findings support the Overlay Model, demonstrating that the VWFA incorporates a new writing system within its existing cortical resources without compromising L1 print processing.
