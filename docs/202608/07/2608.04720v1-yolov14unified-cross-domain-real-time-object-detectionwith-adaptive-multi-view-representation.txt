# Abstract 

Real-time object detectors achieve remarkable accuracy under controlled condi-tions, yet degrade sharply on non-ideal inputs: fisheye distortion, game-rendered characters, aerial viewpoints, and 360 ◦ panoramas. We present YOLOv14 , a unified detection framework addressing these challenges through four synergistic innovations. (1) Deformable Area-Attention (D-AAttn) replaces rigid attention grids with learned 2D deformation fields, enabling adaptive sampling under geo-metric distortion. (2) Game2Real Domain Adaptation aligns rendered-game and photographic feature distributions via Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN) and adversarial domain confusion, allowing game characters are detected as real humans. (3) Multi-View Conditioning injects learned viewpoint embeddings into the backbone with a cross-view contrastive loss that pulls same-class features from different perspectives closer. (4) An Adaptive Augmentation Policy automatically classifies each input’s scene type and routes to optimal augmentations, while a Dy-namicScaleRouter learns per-input feature pyramid weights. Together, YOLOv14 

achieves 49.1 mAP on COCO val2017 at 2.91 ms (T4 GPU), and delivers substan-tial gains on fisheye (+4.1 mAP), panorama (+6.6 mAP), drone (+6.4 mAP), and game-character (+26.1 mAP) benchmarks. 

# 1 Introduction 

The YOLO family [ 20 , 36 , 40 ] has driven real-time object detection from 15 to over 300 frames per second while steadily improving accuracy. However, all existing detectors share a critical unspoken assumption: the input image is captured by an ideal pinhole camera under standard conditions. This assumption breaks in four practical scenarios that arise routinely in real-world deployments: • Geometric distortion. Fisheye and wide-angle lenses introduce radial barrel distortion that shifts and compresses objects near image boundaries, causing standard detectors to miss them entirely. • Cross-domain rendering. Game engines render characters with distinct visual properties— posterization, edge sharpening, saturation boost—that differ significantly from photographs. • Extreme viewpoints. Drone top-down, BEV satellite, and ground-level slanted views present objects at unfamiliar angles and scales. • 360 ◦ panoramas. Equirectangular projections suffer latitude-dependent stretching and boundary discontinuity at 0 ◦/360 ◦.

Preprint. Under review. arXiv:2608.04720v1 [cs.CV] 5 Aug 2026 We propose YOLOv14 , a unified detection framework designed from the ground up for non-ideal imaging conditions. Rather than applying lossy pre-processing corrections, YOLOv14 integrates geometric adaptability into every architectural level. Our contributions are: 1. Deformable Area-Attention (D-AAttn). A learned dense deformation field warps the feature grid before attention computation, enabling adaptive sampling under local distortion (Section 3.2). 2. Game2Real Domain Adaptation. A three-tier mechanism combining data-level stylization, feature-level AdaIN alignment, and objective-level adversarial confusion for robust cross-domain detection (Section 3.3). 3. Multi-View Conditioning. View embeddings with a cross-view NT-Xent loss produce viewpoint-invariant features (Section 3.4). 4. Adaptive Augmentation and Dynamic Routing. Input scene analysis selects optimal augmenta-tions; a gating network learns per-input pyramid scale weights (Section 3.5). 5. State-of-the-art results on standard and four challenging benchmarks with real-time inference (Section 4). 

# 2 Related Work 

Real-time object detection. The YOLO lineage [ 20 , 36 ] has evolved through successive architec-tural and methodological innovations. YOLOv3 [ 35 ] introduced multi-scale prediction and a deeper backbone, while YOLOv4 [ 3 ] systematized bag-of-freebies and bag-of-specials for optimal speed-accuracy trade-offs. YOLOX [ 17 ] advanced anchor-free design with decoupled heads and SimOTA label assignment. YOLOv6-3.0 [ 24 ] proposed a hardware-aware re-parameterization design achieving strong industrial deployment efficiency. YOLOv7 [ 44 ] introduced trainable bag-of-freebies includ-ing planned re-parameterization and coarse-to-fine auxiliary head training, setting new efficiency standards at its time. PP-YOLOE [ 49 ] demonstrated anchor-free efficiency with scale-aware label assignment for industrial applications. YOLO-MS [7] rethought multi-scale representation learning via heterogeneous kernel selection, producing compact yet accurate detectors. Gold-YOLO [ 43 ]proposed a gather-and-distribute mechanism that enhances multi-scale feature fusion beyond conven-tional FPN structures. YOLOv12 [ 40 ] introduced area-attention mechanisms that match CNN speed with Transformer-level modeling, while the very recent YOLOv13 [ 23 ] leverages hypergraph-based adaptive correlation enhancement for cross-location and cross-scale feature fusion. Beyond the YOLO family, anchor-free FCOS [ 41 ] and multi-scale EfficientDet [ 39 ] pioneered efficient one-stage designs. End-to-end detection with Transformers was first demonstrated by DETR [ 5] and later refined by DINO [ 52 ] with improved denoising training. Foundational architectures such as SSD [ 29 ]and FPN [ 28 ] established the multi-scale detection paradigm that underpins modern detectors. We build on these foundations but depart fundamentally by targeting cross-domain non-ideal inputs 

rather than optimizing standard benchmarks. 

Deformable convolutions and attention. DCNv1/v2 [ 11 , 61 ] learn per-kernel-element offsets but require complex engineering to integrate with attention mechanisms. Deformable DETR [ 62 ]extended deformable attention to end-to-end detection, achieving faster convergence. DCN-like architectures have also proven effective in image generation [ 46 ], while Vision Transformer with deformable attention [ 47 ] and CSWin Transformer [ 14 ] explored cross-shaped window attention for efficient high-resolution processing. Our dense-warp-then-convolve strategy achieves similar adaptability with lower complexity, and extends deformability to attention itself. 

Domain adaptation for detection. Prior work [ 6, 15 , 25 ] focuses on synthetic-to-real transfer (e.g., Sim10K to driving). CycleGAN [ 60 ] pioneered unpaired image translation, while MCD [ 38 ] uses classifier discrepancy for unsupervised domain adaptation. Conditional adversarial adaptation [ 30 ]and joint distribution optimal transport [ 12 ] further tighten domain alignment bounds. A compre-hensive survey [ 59 ] systematizes domain generalization strategies. We address the more challenging game-to-real gap, where domain shift involves not only texture but color quantization and geometric differences. 

Multi-view and panoramic detection. Aerial detection [ 13 ] and panoramic methods typically use task-specific architectures. Remote sensing surveys [ 55 ] highlight challenges including orientation and scale variation. WoodScape [ 50 ] provides a multi-task fisheye dataset, while OmniDet [ 22 ]2Figure 1: YOLOv14 system pipeline. addresses surround-view perception. YOLOv14 handles diverse inputs through adaptive conditioning modules within a single unified architecture. 

Data augmentation. Automated augmentation strategies—AutoAugment [ 9], RandAugment [ 10 ], and TrivialAugment [ 34 ]—learn optimal transformation policies. StyleGAN-based augmentation [ 21 ]improves image quality and diversity. MixUp [ 54 ] and CutMix [ 51 ] regularize training through input-level interpolation. For detection specifically, learned augmentation strategies [ 63 ] optimize policies directly for bounding-box tasks. Our AdaptiveAugmentPolicy extends these ideas by routing inputs to scene-specific augmentations. 

Loss functions and training techniques. Beyond standard GIoU [ 37 ] and DFL [ 26 ], subsequent advances include DIoU/CIoU [ 58 ] for faster bounding-box convergence, and SIoU [ 18 ] incorporating angle costs. OTA [ 16 ] frames label assignment as an optimal transport problem, and VarifocalNet [ 53 ]proposes IoU-aware classification for dense detectors. Our detection loss builds on these advances with per-scale weighting via the DynamicScaleRouter. 

Theoretical foundations. Ben-David et al. [ 1 , 2] established the canonical H∆H-divergence framework for domain adaptation bounds. Mansour et al. [ 32 ] extended these results to general loss functions, while Mohri et al. [ 33 ] provide a comprehensive treatment of generalization theory. Zhao et al. [ 56 ] revealed fundamental limitations of invariant representations, and Cortes and Mohri [ 8]analyzed domain adaptation in regression settings. Our theoretical analysis (Section 3.8) builds upon these foundations to derive tight bounds for our specific multi-component architecture.

# 4 Experiments 

4.1 Implementation Details 

We train on COCO train2017 [ 27 ] for 300 epochs using SGD (momentum 0.937, weight decay 

5 × 10 −4), cosine LR schedule (initial 1 × 10 −2), batch size 256 on 4 ×A100 GPUs, input size 640. GameCharacterStylization is applied with probability 0.3. Multi-view training uses synthetic drone/BEV views via RandomPerspectiveDistortion. Inference: single T4 GPU with TensorRT FP16. All reported mAP values are averaged over three independent runs with ± standard deviation.

Medium objects benefit second-most in relative terms (+2.9%), consistent with their higher baseline performance leaving less room for improvement. Notably, the Person category—central to the Game2Real domain adaptation task—shows a moderate +1.8 mAP gain on standard COCO. This is by design: COCO photographs are already well-optimized for person detection, and the Game2Real mechanism (Section 3.3) primarily addresses the much larger domain gap on game-rendered inputs (Table 4: +26 .1 mAP gain on Game benchmark) rather than pushing standard COCO performance further. 

Broader Impact Statement 

Real-time cross-domain object detection carries significant societal implications. Positively, robust detection under fisheye and panoramic conditions improves autonomous vehicle safety, drone-based search-and-rescue, and accessibility. The Game2Real capability enables content moderation and e-sports analytics without separate game-domain models. However, concerns include: Privacy —real-time aerial detection could enable mass surveillance; Dual-use —robust detection of military-styled characters could be repurposed; Bias —domain adaptation does not address demographic bias in person detection. Mitigations we advocate: (1) deploy only with consent and transparency; (2) incorporate differential privacy where feasible; (3) establish ethical use-policy boundaries; (4) release weights under restricted licenses requiring ethical-use attestation. We believe research benefits substantially outweigh risks when deployed with appropriate safeguards.

# 5 Conclusion 

We presented YOLOv14 , a unified real-time detection framework for non-ideal imaging conditions. Through Deformable Area-Attention, Game2Real domain adaptation, Multi-View Conditioning, and Adaptive Augmentation, YOLOv14 achieves state-of-the-art performance across 23 prior detectors spanning CNN, Transformer, and attention-centric families (including the recent YOLOv13), while delivering substantial improvements on fisheye ( +4 .1), panorama ( +6 .6), drone ( +6 .4), and game-character ( +26 .1) benchmarks—all at real-time inference speeds. 

Limitations and Future Work. While YOLOv14 demonstrates substantial improvements, several limitations warrant discussion. 

Dependence on view-type labels. The ViewEmbedding module requires view-type annotations during training. Future work should explore unsupervised view-discovery via clustering or contrastive self-supervised approaches. 

Scene analyzer heuristics. The current AdaptiveAugmentPolicy relies on hand-crafted features (Canny edge density, HSV saturation, contrast variance) that may not generalize to novel scene categories. Learned scene embeddings would provide greater robustness. 

Computational overhead. YOLOv14 adds 27.3% FLOPs over YOLOv12s. While acceptable on server GPUs, this overhead challenges edge devices. Model compression (pruning, distillation, quantization-aware training) is a promising direction. 

Generalization to unseen distortions. Training uses simulated fisheye and perspective transforms. Generalization to catadioptric cameras, underwater refraction, or anamorphic artifacts remains unverified. 

Coverage of game rendering styles. Real games employ diverse pipelines (deferred shading, ray tracing, cel-shading) not fully covered by our stylization. Generative domain transfer could improve coverage. 

Future directions include: (i) online adaptation at test time using unlabelled frames; (ii) extending Game2Real to arbitrary out-of-distribution categories; (iii) multi-modal sensor integration (depth, thermal, events).
