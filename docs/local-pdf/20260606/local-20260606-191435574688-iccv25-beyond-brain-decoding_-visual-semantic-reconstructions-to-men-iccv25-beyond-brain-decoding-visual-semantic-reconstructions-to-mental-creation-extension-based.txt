Abstract
Decoding visual information from fMRI signals is an im-
portant pathway to understand how the brain represents the
world, and is a cutting-edge field of artificial general intelli-
gence. Decoding fMRI should not be limited to reconstruct-
ing visual stimuli, but also further transforming them into
descriptions, creating actions, and even generating unseen
content. We purposefully propose a novel and efficient brain
multimodal architecture, NeuroCreat, which combines the
powerful visual and textual abilities of LLM to capture fine-
grained semantic information from fMRI, transformed it
into embodied implementation of different neural represen-
tations. Specifically, we innovatively designed a brain ex-
pert adaption (BEA) module, effectively capturing common-
alities and individual differences among subjects through
the collaborative learning of shared/routed experts.
In-
spired by human visual working memory, we extracted “cre-
ation” information from higher visual cortex for idea gen-
eration. We further constructed a prompt variant align-
ment module, seamlessly integrates fMRI-visual-semantic-
creation into LLM to achieve flexible incorporation of dif-
ferent semantics in the decoding of neural representations.

Experiments on different fMRI datasets show that Neuro-
Creat achieves SOTA performance on multiple brain decod-
ing tasks. More importantly, we have innovatively achieved
few-shot brain video creation, which opens up a new direc-
tion for demonstrating the brain’s ‘imaginative’ ability.
1. Introduction
“The brain’s lens not only sees but creates realities.” The
human brain is a complex and marvellous organism from
which we have made advances in artificial intelligence by
imitating only some simple mechanisms [47]. The most di-
rect way to understand how the brain works is “seeing” the
information from neural signals, such as fMRI [26], which
encodes the world we perceive every day. Thus the brain be-
comes an experienced creator as we think about and witness
the world [55]. The challenge of mimicking the complexity
and variety of these tasks excites us [31]. We hope that the
visual-semantic decoding model we have constructed will
help more people in the future to demonstrate their creativ-
ity and make progress in practical application areas such as
brain-computer interfaces (BCI) [9].
Humans possess a unique ability to extract semantic con-
cepts from visual information, and the learning of diverse

ment of human imagination. In recent years, with advance-

The brain’s creativity stems from the transmission and
integration of information, we wish to obtain information
about behavioural understanding and verbal interaction of
scenes from fMRI and use fine-grained visual conceptual
representations to enable flexible application of brain de-
coding. Revisiting the existing studies [52], we believe that
it is more consistent with the path of cognitive neuroscience
to take ‘visual working memory’ as the starting point of
creation, and many studies have shown that visual work-
ing memory[15, 32, 33, 42] contains human predictions of
short-term visual concepts. We therefore propose the “cre-
ation” task, an extension of mental creation based on fMRI
signals, which aims to decode human intentional tendencies
towards objects from VWM, thereby guiding the content
generation of “creation”.
We propose NeuroCreat as shown in Fig. 1, a new
framework dedicated to the realisation of creative multi-
modal brain decoding tasks. NeuroCreat consists of three
main components, firstly training the brain adapter that im-
plements shared encoding across multi-subject, Brain Ex-
pert Adaption (BEA) was used to achieve consistency of
voxel information cross-subject, representation of the same
pattern of neural representations and different individuals
is achieved through shared / routed expertise. Secondly,
we proposed Prompt Variant Alignment module, mod-
elled the cognitive mechanism of the human brain and con-
structed a decoding framework by visual structural infor-
mation and fine-grained semantic feature, and represent
“creation” prompt from visual working memory in higher
visual cortex for integration. Finally, we use visual messag-
ing mechanisms to guide multimodal training, using LLM
to integrate fMRI with visual-semantic features to achieve
accurate descriptions of subjects’ visual concepts as well as
creation guidance for different subjects. fMRI was mapped
to the latent embedding space, used Latent Diffusion Model
(LDM) [36], which was combined with the visual semantic
of different subjects to achieve high-precision downstream
visual tasks and creation generation. NeuroCreat accurately
captures and extends visual concepts in the brain, demon-
strating the extended potential of multimodal decoding in
the brain.
In summary, the contributions of our work are threefold:
• We propose NeuroCreat to effectively integrate LLM with
brain decoding, a multiplexed neural decoding model that
achieves unity between LLM and the multimodal visual
decoding of the human brain. Excitingly, we realized a
creative extension based on the visual working memory
of the human brain.
• We innovatively propose the Brain Expert Adaption
(BEA) module, it abstracts visual learning into different
representations of neural modalities, enabling the shar-
ing of visual language and specific neural representation
learning cross-subject. And we designed Prompt Variant
Alignment to realize fine-grained alignment of different
layers of features, which effectively fits the human cogni-
tive mechanism.
• We conducted experiments on different dataset [1, 18],
NeuroCreat achieves quantitative / qualitative SOTA per-
formance in multiple downstream tasks and innovatively
implements creation generation tasks.
2. Related Work
Brain Decoding.
Decoding human visual information
and cognitive concepts from brain signals is a challeng-
ing task. MinD-Vis [8] used a combination of MAE and
diffusion models achieved high-precision semantic extrac-
tion. Mindeye [38] demonstrated that fine-grained voxel
enhancement and deep MLP could achieve high-level re-
construction. There are also some researchers trying to in-
troduce caption of images into visual decoding [12, 27]. As
the research progresses, cross-subject brain decoding be-
came a more worthy research direction [20]. The core of the
problem lies in establishing a shared representation space
to efficiently align the functions of different subjects [46].
Mindeye2 [39] utilises a small portion of fMRI for training
and uses scarce data fine-tuning to achieve it. MindBridge
[49] proposed a biologically-inspired aggregation function
and a cyclic fMRI reconstruction to achieve topic-invariant
representation learning. MindArt [7] enables visual recon-
struction of different styles through graph-matched neural
representations. However, we need to point out that ex-
isting cross-subject decoding methods are in latent space
for the alignment of feature embeddings and mostly use a
single “expert” model, which makes it difficult to learn the
neural representation patterns of subjects between chang-
ing datasets. In this work, we use brain expert adaption to
achieve efficient explicit feature variable learning by intro-
ducing MOE at each layer of the encoder.
Brain-based Multimodal Tasks.
With the development
of MLMM, multimodal information is incorporated in var-
ious research areas. In the field of brain decoding, in ad-

3. Methodology
The NeuroCreat framework is shown in Fig. 2.
It con-
tains three stages: Brain expert adaption (BEA) module
for shared neural embedding learning, the semantic con-
cept representation and fine-grain prompt variant alignment
for LLM, the multi-modal (including “Creation”) decoding
with multi-branch. We will provide more details below.

3.3. Multimodal Visual Task Implementation
In the visual task implementation, the process is shown in
Fig. 2. From the point of view of simulating human vi-
sual cognitive process, NeuroCreat fully considers the flow
of information and fits human low-level visual cognition
(sketch), high-level visual cognition (feature embedding),
and semantic conceptual cognition. And it creatively ex-
tracted the ability of human creation from visual working
memory, generating action prediction after perceiving the
scene, stretching the brain’s creativity.
Specifically, first we use the sketch V ′
i reconstructed
based on the pre-trained VAE as the basis of our recon-
struction, which is also the basis of the creatively generated
action prediction. Secondly, the embedding of our diffu-
sion process is obtained by a streamlined Diffusion U-Net,
which can better achieve zero-shot image classification. We
perform cross-attention coding on the penultimate text en-
coding, embedding it as a time step into the LDM, as high-
level visual feature Zi. Finally, we make full use of the
description information Oi generated by the LLM, which
contains information such as caption, prediction, which we
use as the semantic a priori knowledge for reconstruction.

4. Experiments
4.1. Experimental Setup
We used two publicly available fMRI visual stimulus
datasets: NSD dataset [1] consists fMRI of 8 subjects as
they viewed COCO [24] stimuli.
Each subject viewed
10,000 images, 9,000 special and 1,000 shared images. We
used the 4 subjects completed all scans: S1, S2, S5 and
S7. The training set for each subject contains 8,859 im-
ages and 24,980 fMRI trails, the test set contains 982 im-
ages and 2,770 fMRI trails. We used ‘nsdgeneral’ to prepro-
cess [38, 44] the voxels and used the COCO caption. GOD
dataset [18] consists fMRI of 5 subjects as they viewed Im-
ageNet stimuli, the training set contains 1,200 images (8
images from each of 150 different categories) and test set
consists of 50 images (1 from each of 50 categories). Each
subject resulting in 1,200 and 1,750 fMRI trials. We acquire
caption labels from the GOD-Cap dataset [25], offering two
descriptions for each image.
Architecture. We used the pre-trained CLIP ViT-L/14 &
bigG-14 [35] as visual encoder. LLM model chose LLaMA-
3-8B [29] with input dimension of 4,096, it was fine-tuned
using the LLaMa Adapter [54]. In the encoded output of
the penultimate layer of textual attention, we used 256 patch
tokens associated with fine-tuned features.
Implementation. NeuroCreat is trained on 4 NVIDIA RTX
4090. The global batch size is 256 for 500 epochs of train-
ing, for learning rate tuning we use one-cycle [41] with ini-
tial learning rate of 2 × 10−4. For cross-subject training,
with an alignment dimension of 12,490 per subject, we set
64 of the 256 samples come from the shared expert, the rest
for adaption with 200 epochs. For fine-tune, we used 2 RTX
4090 with batch size of 32. For visual task, we used 50 dif-
fusion steps with intensity of 0.75.
Metrics.
In visual task, we used widely accepted met-
rics, low-level: PixCorr, SSIM [50], AlexNet(2 & 5) [21],
high-level: Inception [43], CLIP [35], EffNet-B [45], and
SwAV [4]. In captioning and interaction task, the metrics
we adopted are: BLEU-k [30], METEOR [3], ROUGE-
L [23], CIDEr [48], SPICE [2], CLIP-S [17]. In creation
task, we used the latest aigcbench [10] (e.g. Flow-Square-
Mean, GenVideo-RefVideo CliP) to evaluate the quality of
creation generation, and we demonstrated fMRI-based cre-
ations using actions in coherent frames.

5. Conclusion
In this study, we propose a novel brain signal decoding
method NeuroCreat, which aims to extend fMRI-based
multimodal vision tasks. Inspired by the distributed pro-
cessing system of the human brain, through brain expert
adaption, we utilize MOE to make each expert a valid sub-
ject brain learner, providing a robust method for unified
brain decoding.
A key innovation of the framework is
prompt variant alignment, which establishes brain-modality
mappings at a fine-grained level, incorporating visual work-
ing memory embedded in higher visual cortex and map-
ping it accurately to “Creation” information, NeuroCreat
enhances the modeling of neural features. The deployment
of NeuroCreat represents a significant brain decoding ad-
vance, providing a new perspective on obtaining meaning-
ful unbiased interpretations from the brain.
